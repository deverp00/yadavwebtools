How to read a candlestick chart

Each candle on the chart represents one interval of time — one hour on the 1h timeframe, one day on the 1D timeframe, and so on. The thick part of the candle (the body) shows the opening and closing price for that interval. The thin lines above and below (the wicks or shadows) show the highest and lowest prices reached during the same period.

A green candle means the price closed higher than it opened — buyers were in control. A red candle means it closed lower — sellers were in control. Long wicks signal that price moved far in one direction but was pushed back before the interval ended, often a sign of rejection at that level.

The pair notation EUR/USD means "how many US dollars does one euro buy?" A rising EUR/USD line means the euro is strengthening against the dollar. For USD/JPY, the reverse — a rising line means the dollar is strengthening against the yen. The first currency is always the base; the second is the quote.

Forex markets are open 24 hours a day, five days a week, from Sunday evening in New York to Friday evening. The most liquid hours are the London and New York sessions overlapping, roughly 8:00 to 12:00 Eastern Time. Spreads are narrowest and volatility is highest during those windows.

The price cards above show the mid-market rate — the midpoint between the buy and sell price at the wholesale level. Your bank or broker will quote a slightly worse rate plus fees. Use the dashboard for information, not as a trading signal. Nothing here is financial advice.
