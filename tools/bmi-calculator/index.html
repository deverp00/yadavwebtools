What BMI measures — and what it does not

Body Mass Index is a simple ratio of weight to height: BMI = weight (kg) / height² (m²). It was originally designed as a quick screening tool for populations, and it remains useful today as one indicator among many. It is not a diagnosis and it does not measure body fat directly.

BMI does not distinguish between muscle, bone, and fat. Athletes and highly muscular people often register as overweight despite low body fat. Older adults and people with low muscle mass may register as normal despite excess fat. Children and pregnant women should not use adult BMI ranges at all.

For a fuller picture, BMI is best combined with waist circumference, body fat percentage, fitness level, family history, and blood markers. If your BMI falls outside the normal range or if you have concerns about your weight, speak with a healthcare professional — this calculator is a starting point, not a verdict.

The healthy weight range shown for your height is calculated from the World Health Organization's normal BMI band of 18.5 to 24.9, converted back into kilograms or pounds for your specific height. Ethnicity-specific thresholds may be lower for some populations; consult a doctor for guidance tailored to you.
