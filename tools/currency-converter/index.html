How currency conversion works

A currency exchange rate expresses how much of one currency is worth one unit of another. If the USD to INR rate is 83.50, then 1 US dollar buys 83.50 Indian rupees. To convert an amount, multiply by the rate. To convert back, divide by the rate — or equivalently, multiply by the inverse pair's rate.

Rates move constantly during trading hours because banks and brokers are continuously matching buy and sell orders. The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buy price (bid) and the sell price (ask). It is the fairest baseline you can compare against, but it is almost never the rate you get as a retail customer.

The historical chart pulls daily reference rates published by the European Central Bank, which update once per working day. This dataset covers around 30 major currencies. When you select a pair outside that set, the chart falls back gracefully and only the live conversion is shown.

Cryptocurrency prices are quoted in USD by aggregators such as CoinGecko and are far more volatile than fiat rates. A price that looks accurate at 10:00 may be off by several percent at 10:01. Use crypto figures with extra caution.
