How EMI is calculated

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay every month for the life of the loan. Each instalment is split between interest on the outstanding balance and repayment of the principal. Early instalments are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal.

The formula is EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the total number of monthly payments (years × 12). The tool uses double-precision arithmetic and rounds only the final EMI to the nearest rupee, so the schedule matches what banks publish within a few rupees.

The amortization table shows, for each completed year, how much of your payments went toward interest, how much reduced the principal, and what balance remained at year-end. The final row shows totals. You will notice that in the first few years the interest portion is far larger than the principal portion — this is normal and is the reason prepaying early saves so much interest.

The donut chart visualises the split between principal and interest across the whole loan. A small shift in the interest rate or tenure dramatically changes that split — try moving the sliders to see how sensitive the total cost is to each input. All figures are estimates for planning; actual EMI depends on lender-specific fees, insurance, and processing charges.
