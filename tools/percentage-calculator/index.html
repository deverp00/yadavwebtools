The five percentage problems, explained

1. X% of Y — the most common ask. Multiply Y by X and divide by 100. Example: 15% of 200 = (15 × 200) / 100 = 30. Useful for tips, discounts, tax, interest, and margins.

2. X is what % of Y — flip the question. Divide X by Y and multiply by 100. Example: 30 is 20% of 150 because (30 / 150) × 100 = 20. Useful for score percentages, contribution ratios, and coverage metrics.

3. % change from A to B — measures how much something grew or shrank. Take the difference, divide by the original value, then multiply by 100. Example: from 80 to 100 is a 25% increase because ((100 − 80) / 80) × 100 = 25. Going from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease, not a 25% one — the denominator changes.

4. Increase or decrease by X% — apply a percentage change to a starting value. Increase: value × (1 + X/100). Decrease: value × (1 − X/100). Example: 500 increased by 20% = 500 × 1.20 = 600.

5. Reverse percentage — you know the final value and the percentage that was applied, and want to find the original. For an increase, original = final / (1 + X/100). For a decrease, original = final / (1 − X/100). Example: a 20% increase that ended at 120 means the original was 120 / 1.20 = 100.

All five modes are computed locally in your browser with full floating-point precision. Results are shown with up to ten significant digits and always include the exact formula used, so you can verify each answer by hand.
