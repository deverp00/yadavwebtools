How GST is calculated

GST is a destination-based tax applied on the supply of goods and services. When you have a base (pre-tax) amount and want to add GST, the tax is a simple percentage of the base: GST = base × rate / 100, and total = base + GST.

When you have a total that already includes GST and want to extract the base, the maths reverses: base = total / (1 + rate / 100), and GST = total − base. This is the formula used by every GST invoice and return, and it avoids the common mistake of subtracting the rate percentage from the inclusive amount.

For intra-state supplies (buyer and seller in the same state), the tax is split equally between the Centre and the State as CGST and SGST. For inter-state supplies (buyer and seller in different states), the whole amount is charged as IGST. The tax total is always the same; only the split changes.

GST rates in India currently fall into the slabs shown above. Some goods attract cess on top of the highest slab (for example, large cars and tobacco), which is not included in this calculator. The figures shown are estimates for planning purposes and do not replace a tax invoice or professional advice.
